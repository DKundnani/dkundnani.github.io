About me

I am a computational biologist with a molecular-biology foundation — which means I have pipetted the samples that generate the data I now analyse. That combination shapes how I work: I trust a result more when I understand the assay that produced it.

My Ph.D. at Georgia Tech focused on the discovery and validation of ribonucleotide incorporation in the human genome as a novel epigenomic phenomenon. Along the way I characterised distinctive ribonucleotide embedment features in mutants associated with a rare genetic disorder, studied enzyme associations across large public datasets, and released tailored software packages for the field. That work was recognised with the Mark Borodovsky Prize for Excellence in Bioinformatics.

Before Georgia Tech, I developed, optimised, and validated multiplex immunoassays for protein biomarkers in patient blood samples for the early detection of multiple cancers at the Hanash Lab, MD Anderson Cancer Center. That team's work on lung cancer risk-assessment biomarkers was recognised institution-wide.
